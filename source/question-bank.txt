At the same grade, what happens to contained copper when rock mass doubles?|It doubles ~ It halves ~ It stays fixed ~ It increases tenfold|Contained metal is proportional to mass when grade stays fixed.
If rock contains 8 tonnes of copper and processing recovers 75%, how much copper is recovered?|6 tonnes ~ 8 tonnes ~ 2 tonnes ~ 10.67 tonnes|Recovery applies to contained metal: 8 × 0.75 = 6 tonnes.
Equal masses of rock grade 1% and 3% copper. What is their combined grade?|2% ~ 4% ~ 1.5% ~ 3%|Equal masses give equal weights: (1 + 3) / 2 = 2%.

## Variograms and kriging
What does a semivariogram plot relate?|Separation distance or direction to average half-squared differences ~ Model parameter count to token count ~ Grade directly to market price ~ Only borehole ID to filename|Pairs are grouped by spatial separation to estimate how similarity changes across space.
If all paired observations in a distance bin are equal within each pair, what is empirical semivariance in that bin?|0 ~ 1 ~ The pair count ~ Infinity|Every paired difference is zero, so all squared contributions vanish.
One pair has values 2 and 6. What is its semivariance contribution when it is the only pair in the bin?|8 ~ 4 ~ 16 ~ 2|Square the difference 4 and divide by 2: 16/2=8.
For a fixed set of observation pairs, every value difference doubles. What happens to the estimated semivariance?|It becomes four times as large ~ It doubles ~ It stays unchanged ~ It is halved|Semivariance averages squared pair differences with the same denominator. Doubling each difference multiplies every squared term by four.
At the same separation distance, east–west sample pairs differ little, while north–south pairs differ much more. What extra information should a spatial-dependence model consider?|The direction between samples ~ Only the number of decimal places ~ Only the sample filenames ~ No spatial information|The observed differences depend on direction as well as distance. A model may need to represent that directional structure.
For ordinary kriging, what must the weights sum to?|1 ~ 0 ~ The sample count ~ The target grade|The unit-sum condition preserves an unknown constant mean under the model.
Given kriging weights 0.4 and 0.6 and corresponding observations 3 and 8, what is the estimate?|6 ~ 5.5 ~ 11 ~ 4.4|The weighted estimate is 0.4×3+0.6×8 = 1.2+4.8 = 6.
Fixed ordinary-kriging weights have a total of 1. If every sample value increases by 2, how much does the weighted estimate increase?|2 ~ 0 ~ The number of samples ~ 4|The change is Σwᵢ×2 = 2Σwᵢ = 2. The sum-to-one condition preserves a common shift of all sample values.
A kriging uncertainty estimate is calculated from a fitted spatial model. Does that number automatically include all errors in the model assumptions and sample measurements?|No ~ Yes ~ Only under a smooth variogram ~ Only with exact coordinates|It is conditional on the spatial model and setup; model misspecification, uncertain parameters, and sampling biases need separate consideration.

## Mineral processing and recovery
How does concentrate grade differ from metal recovery?|Grade is concentration in the concentrate; recovery is the fraction of feed metal captured ~ Both always have the same numerical value ~ Grade is total feed mass ~ Recovery is tailings volume only|The denominators differ: concentrate mass for grade, feed contained metal for recovery.

In a steady two-output dry-mass balance, feed is 250 tonnes and concentrate is 40 tonnes. What is tailings mass?|210 tonnes ~ 290 tonnes ~ 40 tonnes ~ 6.25 tonnes|Conservation gives feed = concentrate + tailings. Thus tailings = 250−40 = 210 tonnes.

Does a higher concentrate grade necessarily mean higher metal recovery?|No ~ Yes ~ Only for copper ~ Only for small plants|A tighter separation may produce a purer concentrate while sending more valuable metal to tailings.
A wet mass includes water; a dry mass excludes it. Grades are stated on a specified mass basis. Why use compatible mass and grade bases for feed and concentrate?|Water content can distort the mass and metal balance ~ Moisture determines the atomic mass of copper ~ All wet masses equal dry masses ~ Grades are independent of their stated basis|Multiplying a dry-basis grade by an uncorrected wet mass includes water as though it carried that grade. Convert the measurements to compatible bases before balancing metal.
If contained feed metal is 10 tonnes and recovery is 70%, what metal mass reaches concentrate?|7 tonnes ~ 3 tonnes ~ 70 tonnes ~ 0.7 tonnes|Multiply contained metal by the recovered fraction: 10×0.70=7 tonnes.
What does a calculated recovery above 100% suggest in a closed, steady metal balance?|A problem with measurements, bases, time alignment, or balance assumptions ~ Creation of additional metal by separation ~ Proof of a perfect model ~ A normal requirement of high concentrate grade|Account for inventory and other streams when applicable, and check the data before accepting an impossible closed-balance result.
